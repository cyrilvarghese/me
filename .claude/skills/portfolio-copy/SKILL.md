# Portfolio copy

House voice for everything a visitor reads. CLAUDE.md carries the banned
words; this skill carries the shape good copy takes.

## Rule 0 — Who is reading

**A design recruiter, a hiring manager, or another product designer.** Not
a specialist in the case study's industry. They are scanning to find out
how this person thinks, and they will not stop to decode a term.

So the copy explains the *design decision*, never the domain. A case study
set in a hospital, a bank or a warehouse still reads as product work: the
constraint, the choice, the reason. Domain nouns are fine where a general
reader already knows them; domain *jargon* is not, and it hides the
decision rather than proving expertise.

| Wrote (rejected) | Shipped |
|---|---|
| The list carries distractors, so every candidate has to be marked primary, differential, or ruled out. | Plausible wrong answers are mixed in, so every option has to be judged rather than skimmed. |
| A lab test comes back as stained slides, so the reading is still the student's work. | Results come back as images to interpret, so the judgement stays with the student. |

The test: would a designer who has never worked in this industry
understand what was decided, and why? If the sentence only lands for
someone inside the domain, it is not doing its job.

Two things this does **not** license. It is not dumbing down — the
decision keeps its full precision, only the vocabulary changes. And it
never invents: a claim absent from the source material does not become
true by being restated in plainer words.

## Rule 1 — Clarity first, never performed cleverness

Copy is not trying to appear smart. It says the thing plainly, and it is
allowed to be fun — warmth and play are welcome *after* the sentence is
already clear, never as a substitute for it. If a line makes the reader
feel the writer's intelligence before the content lands, rewrite it.

## Titles name their content

A title is the fact of the section in the reader's own words: subject +
what's true about it. Scannable in one pass, understood before the body
is read. It is not a setup, a tease, a two-beat rhetorical figure, or a
claim the body then has to earn.

| Wrote (rejected) | Shipped |
|---|---|
| Half the day is selling. The other half is keeping. | The day is half selling, half servicing. |

The test: could a visitor skim only the titles and still tell someone
what the page says? A title that needs its body to make sense fails.

## Applying it

- Draft the plain version first. Only then ask if a lighter touch earns
  its place — most of the time the plain version ships.
- Cut any word the reader must re-read: inversions, withheld subjects,
  em-dash pivots that delay the point.
- Read the section as a scanner would — titles and bold only — and check
  the story still tells itself.
